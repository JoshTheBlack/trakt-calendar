# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. Format loosely follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/).

## 🏷️ [1.2.5] - 2026-09-22

### Changed
- 🔗 **The details panel links out to each service that knows the title.** One button per service, wearing that service's name, the way a calendar card already did — and on the tracker there was previously no way at all to get from a show to the service holding its data. A title only one of them lists gets one button rather than a second that goes nowhere, and each link is spelled with that service's own name for the show wherever it is known.
- 🎬 **The panel a card opens shows the same things wherever you open it.** The age rating was missing from shared calendars, the IMDb score from everywhere but your own calendar, and the poster from one of the three — not because those pages had nothing to say, but because each page drew that panel for itself and a field added to one never reached the others. There is one panel now, so anything it learns to show it shows everywhere at once.
- 📺 **Every season of a show is one press away from the panel.** A row of season buttons sits under the service links, so "what was the last one about" no longer means going and looking it up. A season is fetched the first time you open its tab and stays put after that, so moving back and forth costs nothing. A shared calendar has no season buttons: it publishes one month, so most seasons are not its to show.
- 🎭 **The panel tells you about the season as well as the show.** What the show is stays where it always was, next to the badges; below the season buttons you now get what *that season* is about, who is in it, and a season with a name of its own — Murder House, Night Country, Candle Cove — says so. The cast was quietly wrong before: it listed the whole show's, so season 1 of *True Detective* named two actors who do not appear until season 4.
- ↩️ **Taking a title off a rankings board is one press, and putting it back is another.** It no longer stops to ask, because it is no longer hard to undo: the offer to put the title back appears the moment it goes, and a board keeps the last 25 titles taken off it for 30 days in a "Recently removed" list beside the pool. Any one of them goes back with a press — into the tier it was in, at the place it held, or into the pool if that tier has since been deleted. Undo used to be a single button that vanished after nine seconds or the moment you reloaded, and could only ever bring back the title you had just removed. A title you have since added back by hand is left where you put it, and you are told so rather than given a second copy.
- 🗃️ **Deleting a tier can be undone after a reload, or from another tab.** The Undo that appears when you delete one puts the tier back as it was — its name, color, priority and place on the board, with its titles in the order they sat — and a board keeps the last 10 tiers you deleted for 30 days in the same "Recently removed" list, beside the titles. Undo used to vanish after nine seconds or the moment you reloaded, and was gone for good if you missed it. Anything you have done with those titles since is respected: one you have moved into another tier stays there, and putting the tier back tells you exactly how many of its titles came back with it and what happened to the rest, rather than quietly bringing back fewer than you expected. Applying the template after deleting a tier no longer costs you the undo either — the tier's titles still waiting in the pool go back into it.
- 📈 **A rankings board holds up to 2,000 titles, and shows up to 1,000 of them without opening a tier.** Both limits doubled or better — a board used to top out at 1,000 titles and drew its tiers closed past 200. Each title's row is about half the size it was, which is what makes room for it: a board with a thousand titles on show redraws after a change in roughly two thirds of the time it took before. The ⇄ menu on a row is fetched the first time you open it rather than sent with every row, so its first opening takes a moment longer. A board past 1,000 tiered titles still draws its tiers closed, each opening when you ask for it.
- 🗂️ **Changing a rankings board no longer reloads the page.** Adding a tier, applying the template, writing a tier's settings and renaming a board each threw the whole page away and fetched it again; they redraw the board in place now. Anything you dragged a moment before is written before the change is sent, so it is never the thing that gets lost.
- 🖼️ **Everything the export dialog offers is decided before it opens.** The tier list, the formats with their size limits, your saved pictures and the name it fills in used to be assembled after you pressed Export; they arrive with the page. The size it reports is now the one the renderer itself works out rather than a separate calculation kept in step by hand, and a picture uploaded from the dialog is named as you add it instead of afterwards.
- 🔍 **A shared calendar fills in a card's details even when you are the first person to open it.** A link used to show only what its owner had already looked at themselves, so most cards opened on an empty panel with a title and nothing else. The page looks the title up now, and because that description is the same for everybody, one look-up serves every person who opens the link. It can only ever look up something the calendar in front of you is already showing — a link can never be used to make an account go and ask about anything else.

- 🎚️ **Library buttons arrive already showing what is in Sonarr, Radarr and Seerr.** An administrator's add buttons used to appear plain and change a moment after the page loaded, once it had asked; they are drawn already marked now, on every card and in a title's panel. What they no longer do is change while you look at them: a service going down, or a title added in Sonarr directly, shows the next time the page loads — and adding to a service that has gone down since still tells you it failed.
- ➕ **Add all adds the whole month, and asks first.** It said it added every watching title this month, and it added only the ones on days the page had already loaded as you scrolled. It now adds every title the month shows that you are watching — your filters still apply — once each, and leaves out anything already in Sonarr or Radarr. Pressing it counts what it would add and says so, with the adding itself behind a second press and a Cancel that changes nothing.
- ⚙️ **Settings is in the menu on every page.** An administrator used to have to go to the calendar to open it; it sits in the ☰ menu wherever you are now, and still only for administrators.
- 🔑 **Closing Settings no longer throws away a Trakt pairing code.** The code you were about to enter on trakt.tv is still there when you open Settings again, and approving it is picked up as soon as the panel is open — pressing Cancel is what abandons it.
- 🎚️ **Saving your filters redraws the calendar instead of reloading the page.** The Filters panel used to throw the whole page away and fetch it again, which meant the fonts, the stylesheet and every script came back down with it and your place on the page was lost. The month, its counts and the day strip are redrawn in place now, and the panel closes itself when the save lands.
- 🏷️ **A card's badges show what you are already filtering on.** Press a genre, country, rating or network on one card and every card showing it is marked at once — and they stay marked when the page loads again, which they never did: the colour was your browser remembering the press, so it vanished on the next load while the filter went on applying. A badge the app cannot act on now says so instead of looking as though it worked.
- 🎚️ **The Filters panel opens on what is stored, every time.** It is drawn when you press 🎚️ rather than shipped with the page, so a chip you cycled and did not save, or a filter changed in another tab, can no longer be sitting there the next time you open it. Typing a name the chips do not offer adds it straight away — a comma-separated list adds one chip each — and closing without saving drops it, as it always did.
- 👁️ **Marking a show as not-watching now marks it everywhere at once.** The mark applies to the whole month, and the page used to work out for itself which cards to cross out and what the counts should become — so a day you had not scrolled to yet, a count it had to guess at, and a second tab all had their own idea of what was marked. The server draws the answer now: every card for that title on the month comes back crossed out together, with the two counts and the day strip, whether or not you had scrolled that far. The cost is that the ribbon appears when the save lands rather than the instant you press, and a save that fails can no longer leave a card looking marked when nothing was stored.
- 🔁 **A card's panel opens on what the card is showing.** Where two services describe a title differently, the name, the picture and the description you are looking at — flipped or not — are what the panel draws, rather than whichever service happened to answer the lookup.
- 👤 **Your account page changes in place.** Reordering services, choosing, refreshing or forgetting a picture, retiring a service's history and unlinking an account each reloaded the whole page; they redraw the part of the page they change now, and a problem is said beside the control that caused it.
- 🛠️ **The admin page asks and answers on the page.** Resetting a password, deleting an account and every "are you sure" used to be browser pop-ups; they open beside the button now, a temporary password is shown in the page ready to copy, an invite's redemptions open under its row, and every action says what it did without reloading. Times are shown in your account's timezone, like everywhere else.
- 🎬 **Signing in with Plex survives a closed or blocked window.** Closing the Plex window used to end the sign-in; now the page keeps waiting and offers a link to open the Plex page again. A browser that blocks the window is told so, with the same link, and approving from that tab signs you in just the same. Waiting gives up after three minutes and says so.

### Added
- 🧺 **Pick titles anywhere on a rankings board, and drag them all at once.** Ctrl-click — ⌘-click on a Mac — picks a title in the pool or in any open tier, and shift-click picks a run of them within one list. Drag any picked title and every picked title comes with it, from wherever each one was, and they stay picked where they land so you can carry them on. The bar that counts them stays at the foot of the window and can send them into any tier or back to the pool. A plain click still picks nothing, so clicking about a board can never cost you a selection you have built up. On a board large enough to draw its tiers closed, a tier's titles can be picked once it is opened.
- 🎞️ **A title on a rankings board opens the same details panel as everywhere else.** The ⓘ on a row brings up its description, cast, trailer and a button for every season — a board holds titles rather than seasons, so it opens on the first and the rest are a press away. A title added without anything a service can look it up by still opens, on what the board itself knows about it, and says why there are no episodes to show.
- 👀 **A calendar you share shows how far along you are.** Anyone holding one of your links sees the same watched marks your own calendar shows, on the same episode lists — which is most of the point of sharing a calendar in the first place. A season you have watched nothing of shows no marks at all. You can turn the whole thing off from your account page, and turning it off applies to every link you have already shared, straight away.
- ✅ **Your own watched episodes are marked on the calendar's panel too.** Opening a card shows which episodes of that season you have seen, and how many — the same marks, from the same record, as the page that has always shown them. It is read from what this app has already stored, so it costs nothing and asks nobody. A show you have watched nothing of still gets the marks, unfilled, because each one is also the link to that episode on the service that would record it.

### Fixed
- 🧮 **Moving titles out of a long pool no longer loses track of the rest of it.** The pool shows its titles sixty at a time, and each new batch was asked for by position — "the sixty starting at the 120th" — while the page kept saving what you moved. Move a batch out and the next one slid past titles you never saw, so the pool looked empty while titles were still in it, and they turned up again after a reload as if your moves had been undone. Put titles back into the pool and the next batch repeated ones already on screen, and every save after that was refused for naming a title twice, until a reload lost whatever had not been saved. Each batch is now "the titles after the last one shown", and a batch never draws a title the page already has.
- 🗂️ **The whole pool scrolls on a rankings board, and the bulk-move bar is where you can see it.** A pool longer than the window was cut off at the bottom of the panel with no way to scroll to the rest: on a board with sixty titles, eight were reachable and everything below them was drawn but unreachable, including the bar that appears when you pick titles — so ticking two and looking for somewhere to send them found nothing. The rows scroll now, and the bar stays in view at the foot of the window for as long as anything is picked.
- 👁️ **A show you marked as not-watching can be un-marked again.** Marks made before the app settled on one way of naming a title were filed under whichever name that title went by on the day — and while the calendar still read them correctly, pressing the eye to undo one looked for a different name and removed nothing. The show simply stayed crossed out, which is why it appeared to work until the next reload. On one real account that was 909 of 1,038 marks. Undoing a mark now clears every name the title has ever been filed under, so the ones that were stuck come free the first time you press them. Where one name covers two different shows — the same title made twice, which does happen — un-marking clears both, and marking either afterwards keeps them apart.
- ⚡ **Opening a busy month, and marking a show on one, are both much faster.** A month with a few thousand airings was being assembled from scratch for every single thing asked of it — opening it, switching to it and back, marking one show, changing how it is drawn — although none of that work depends on which shows you are watching. It is now worked out once and reused for a short while, and thrown away the moment anything it was built from changes. On a real September of some 2,300 airings the assembly went from about three seconds to a fiftieth of one.
- 📡 **A network badge stops asking for a logo that is not there.** Every badge on the calendar pointed at a logo and quietly coped when there was none — which meant a failed request per network without one, on every single load: ninety-nine of them on a real month, all competing with the rest of the page for the handful of connections a browser will open. The page now knows which logos exist before it draws anything, and a network without one simply shows its emoji. Logos that have never been fetched are built quietly in the background and appear next time, rather than holding the page up.
- 📄 **A busy month sends a far smaller page to start with.** The calendar drew its first five days into the page itself and fetched the rest as you scrolled — which is a few dozen cards on most calendars and 462 on All Episodes, where a month of daily serials puts a hundred titles on a single day. It now fills the page up to a budget of cards rather than a count of days, so the first thing your browser receives is about a quarter of the size it was and the rest arrives exactly as before.
- 🥚 **Adding to a month you are looking back at records it on that month.** The add and film searches were not told which month you had open, so everything after them assumed today: adding a finished show to a past month ran the checks meant for the month in progress and put it on your list instead of recording it as finished, and adding a film redrew the current month over the one you were reading. Both now act on the month on screen.
- 🎬 **A trailer stops when you close the panel, however you close it.** Pressing Escape hid the box and left the video playing behind it — audible, with nothing on screen to stop it. Every way of dismissing any panel in the app now goes through the same shutdown, so this cannot come back for one of them and not the others.
- 🪟 **Every panel closes the same three ways — the button, a click outside it, and Escape — and keeps the keyboard inside it while it is open.** Which of those worked used to depend on which panel you had open: some ignored a click outside, and on one page Escape did nothing at all. There was no reason behind the differences; they were written down one panel at a time. Every panel in the app is now the browser's own kind of panel, so all three work everywhere, Tab cannot wander out of an open panel into the page behind it, and closing one puts you back on whatever you opened it with.
- 📋 **A title pasted into the calendar search is searched for.** The box only noticed keys being pressed, so a title pasted with the mouse — or cleared with the browser's own button — left the results showing whatever had been there before, until you typed something more. It now notices every change to what is in the box, however it got there.
- 📺 **The last service filling your calendar always looks like the last one.** The Filters panel greys out whichever service is the only one still on — but only on a page loaded from scratch. Reach the calendar by any link inside the app, which is the ordinary way, and it looked like an ordinary tick you were free to clear; the click was refused after the fact instead. It is now greyed and unclickable however you arrived, with the reason already printed under the switches.
- 🚫 **A show Sonarr can't take no longer offers to add it.** A title with no TVDB id (or, for a film, no TMDB id) drew an ordinary Add button that could only ever fail with a message saying so. Its button is greyed out now and says why before you press it.
- 🖼️ **A tier's row of posters keeps up with what is actually in the tier.** Dragging a title out of one, or sending it somewhere with the ⇄ menu, left its poster sitting in the tier's header — and a poster for the title that arrived never appeared — until the page was reloaded.
- 🚧 **Changing the content floor now changes the calendar at once.** Genres, countries, certifications and networks excluded in Settings were applied only when a week of calendar data was fetched, so anything already loaded kept showing what had just been excluded until it aged out on its own — up to a day or more. Those exclusions now also apply as the calendar is read, so the change is visible immediately, on months that are already loaded as well as on new ones. Nothing is thrown away: undoing an exclusion brings the titles straight back.
- 🚧 **The Networks line in Settings actually filters the calendar now.** It sat with the other content-floor fields and only ever reached shared links, so an instance-wide network exclusion did nothing for anyone signed in.
- 📺 **Loading Sonarr or Radarr profiles asks the server you just typed.** With an API key already saved, a new URL typed beside it was ignored and the options came from the old server; the saved key is now only used for the half you left blank.
- 🔗 **The Share panel no longer says switching links turns the others off.** It never did — every link you have shared keeps working — and the panel now says so.
- 👆 **The Undo on a rankings board's pop-up is easy to read.** It was drawn in the page's dim button colours on top of the pop-up's green, which made it easiest to miss at exactly the moment you wanted it.
- 🍪 **The sign-in page's cookie warning is right about localhost.** It warned whenever a secure-cookie instance was opened over http://, including on http://localhost, where browsers keep the cookie and sign-in works. It now asks your browser whether it actually kept one, and warns only when it did not.
- 🎬 **The Plex button says so when Plex can't be reached.** A dropped connection to plex.tv left the button doing nothing at all; it now says sign-in with Plex isn't available right now.
- ✏️ **A field redrawn after saving shows what was saved.** Saving your display name could leave the box empty even though the name was stored — and anywhere else in the app a saved field is redrawn could do the same.

### 🥚
- The key sequence no longer throws you out of the page it opens. Once both had been visited in one session, pressing it on the second went back to the first; there is one listener now and it reads which page it is on.
- What a row opens is the same panel the rest of the app opens, drawn by the same thing, so a score it never had room to show is simply there, and a picture with it.
- A row's panel offers every item the thing has, not just the one the row is for, so the rest can be read without going anywhere. A season is fetched the first time it is opened, and one that never had a row of its own opens just the same.
- Every line now says which of them can be asked about that title, not only which of them said yes. A line where one has it and the other has not published it yet used to look exactly like a line only one of them was ever consulted about. Where only one of them knows the title at all, it says nothing — there is nothing to tell apart.
- The list and the text it produces finally agree about where a title goes. A title separated from its leading word by an unusual space, and any title with an accent in it, sat in one place on the list and another in the text — the two were ordered by different rules, and the text was the one that was right.
- What it records is what the service said, not what the page had on screen. Picking something out of a search used to hand its identity back to be filed; it now looks that choice up again in the answer it already has, and says so politely if that answer has aged out. A wrong identity, once filed, is not something it can work out for itself later.
- The phrase you type before replacing everything is checked where it counts, not only in front of you.
- Everything on it is drawn in one place now — the sections, the rows, the questions above them, both blocks of text and every button on a row — so what you are looking at and what you are about to paste cannot come from two different ideas of the month. The buttons look and behave as they did.
- The link at the bottom of the first block is plain now, so where you paste it the picture comes with it. It used to be written in the way that tells the app not to draw one.
- Checking what filling in past months would add no longer claims a month it deliberately never read is empty. A month it leaves alone now says that it did, a row with nothing behind it has stopped offering to open, and where there is nothing to write it no longer offers to write it.

### Under the hood
- 🖼️ **One panel, built once, instead of three that had quietly stopped matching.** The same box of details was assembled separately for each of the three places it opens, in the browser, from the same answer — so two of them had drifted apart in what they showed and nobody could see it from any one page. It is built on the server now, in one place, and every page that opens it — the rankings board included — gets that same one, saying only what is genuinely different there, such as whether your own viewing is marked on it.
- 🪶 **A rankings board is drawn and changed in one place, and that place is not your browser.** Every action on it that is not a drag — making, renaming, cloning and deleting boards, adding tiers and applying the template, writing a tier's settings, taking a title off, moving a selection, importing, seeding, the export options and restoring a backup — used to edit a copy of the board held in the page and then write the whole arrangement back. Each one is a control that sends itself now and is answered with the board as the server holds it, so there is one idea of what is on a board rather than two that could disagree. Every row used to carry a hidden copy of its whole title, on every row of every board, only so that undoing a removal could put it back; that record lives in the board's removal history now. Search results are drawn by the server rather than assembled in your browser, and adding one tells the server which result you picked instead of passing the title along — so what a board stores is what the service itself said about the title, and the search you just ran is reused rather than asked for again. What the page still does is what only a browser can: dragging, the keyboard and modifier-key ways of moving a title, where a menu opens, the preview's zoom, reading a file you chose, and saving the finished picture.
- 📚 **A season's own description and cast are asked for by name.** They were always published — they were simply never requested, so every season of a show was described with the same paragraph and the same list of actors. Where a service has nothing to say per season, the show's own details are shown instead, so nothing is emptier than it was.
- 🎬 **Both services describe a title with the same set of facts.** One of them answered with three fewer than the other, so a detail could go missing from a card for no better reason than which service happened to describe it. Each answers the full set now, filled in where it has something to say and left blank where it genuinely has nothing.
- 🧭 **The engine behind moving around the app is a major version newer.** Every link between pages, the day blocks that fill themselves in as you scroll, the board's tiers as you open them and the calendar search as you type all run on it. Nothing you do should look different — going Back still asks the server for the page as it stands now rather than handing you back the copy you walked away from, which is what stops a board you have rearranged since from refusing your next change.
- 📨 **A button or a form can send itself.** Every write this app accepts has to arrive in one particular shape, so each panel's fields used to be read out and sent by hand. The piece that lets a plain button or form send its own contents in that shape now ships with every page, and everything that writes uses it: the calendar's add-to-library buttons and card badges, its Filters panel, every control on a rankings board, your account's saved pictures, the Settings and Share panels, and signing out.
- ⚙️ **The Settings, Share and Filters panels are drawn by the server, whole.** Both used to open empty and be filled in by your browser from a separate request — every field, every hint, the encryption section's state and the Share panel's list of services — and each warning was worked out a second time in the browser from facts the server already had. They arrive filled in now, every control sends itself, and what a setting means (a number, a switch, a size typed in megabytes) is decided in one place on the server rather than field by field in the page. A saved credential is still never shown: its field says whether one is stored and offers to clear it. Trakt's pairing code is held on the server too, instead of in the page alongside a client secret it re-sent every few seconds. The Filters panel joined them: what a filter *is* — which answers a chip cycles through, whether two spellings of a network are one network, what clearing a tab means — was written out a second time in the browser, and none of it is now.
- 🧹 **Less of the calendar is put together in your browser.** The line under a card saying how many episodes its season has arrives already drawn, fetched as the card scrolls into view. A calendar card, a shared calendar's card and a rankings row all open the details panel the same way, from what the page already says. A day that loads as you scroll arrives with its ✨ new marks rather than having them added once it lands. How wide a day is, whether hiding empties it, and which of the three answers a filter chip is showing are all the stylesheet's now, so they are right at the moment anything changes them instead of a frame later. And adding a title to Sonarr, Radarr or Seerr is the button itself: every copy of it on the page comes back marked from the one answer, as does every copy of a filter badge. Two of the calendar's scripts are gone entirely with the panel and the badges, and the page carries no filtering code at all.
- 🎬 **How your calendar is drawn is decided in one place, and it is not your browser.** Card style, day packing and hiding what you are not watching each used to flip a class on the page and then, separately, save; now one press does both — the server stores it and says what the page should look like, so what you are seeing and what is saved can no longer disagree. Hiding also redraws the day strip with it, so a day it empties stops offering to jump there. The cost is that the change lands when the save does rather than a moment before it.
- 🔎 **Following a search result lands on the card, and stays there.** Where it lands is now decided by the page itself rather than by a script that scrolled, watched to see whether anything had moved it, and scrolled again — so the landing is settled the moment you arrive instead of being nudged for up to twelve seconds afterwards. The card is also no longer left with a green edge if you wander off before the highlight finishes.
- ✂️ **The last audit of what still has to run in your browser, and five of its files came out empty.** Every remaining line was read against the question of whether markup, a stylesheet rule, a template or the server could do it instead. Both header menus, all sixteen panels, the menu that moves a title between tiers, flipping a card between the services that described it, and the four view preferences are the browser's own behaviour or the server's answer now; the header's script, the calendar's view script, its card-flip script and the script behind following a search result are gone entirely, and one page is left running nothing of its own at all. A title too long for the space it sits in still scrolls when you point at it, but nothing measures it any more — the stylesheet works out how far it has to go from the width of the space itself. A card no longer carries a hidden copy of every value it might show — on one real database that copy ran to 1,790 characters per card — because both values are simply drawn, and the page no longer keeps its own tally of the month. What is left is written down: every script this app serves has to open by saying what it could not have been instead, and a test refuses one that does not.
- 🔐 **Signing in, setting up, your account, the admin page and encryption recovery carry no page scripts of their own.** Each was a script over a set of requests, together several hundred lines; every form and button on them now sends itself and is answered with what the server draws. Enter submits a sign-in form because it is a form, the cookie warning comes from the server, and a recovery key is shown in the answer that generated it and nowhere else. What is left for a browser on these pages is opening the Plex window inside your click, reading a picture you chose, and copying a value — the only three things a page cannot do for itself.

## 🏷️ [1.2.4] - 2026-09-22

### 🥚
- Something you had lined up but not yet started was being reported as taken away from you. One of them keeps those apart from the ones you have begun, and only half of that was ever being asked for — so a list you had been building read as a list somebody had emptied. All of it is asked for now. And when a great many go at once it says so plainly, which is the part that was missing: there was nothing to read while two thirds of it was being marked.
- A month that is over is a set of records you can edit one at a time, and this round makes that true in the four places it was not. Forgetting one film took the others with it, because the list was being worked out again from scratch instead of having one entry taken out of it. Filling a month in from your history refused any month that had anything on it at all, which is a fine rule until one wrong row makes a month impossible to fix — it now writes what it found over the entries for the same things, adds the ones that were not there, and leaves everything else alone, saying up front how many of each. And adding something to a month that has finished no longer asks whether you are starting over: you named the month, which is the answer. That last one used to depend on whether the month had been tidied away, so a month nobody had been around to tidy behaved like this one.

## 🏷️ [1.2.3] - 2026-09-09

### 🥚
- Something added by hand arrived not knowing what one of them calls it, so every link to it afterwards went by number instead of by name. It keeps the name now — and so does what the other one hands back, which was quietly dropping it in the same way.
- Opening something up asks one of them about it and is told the name in the answer. It used to show the blank it already had and throw that answer away; now it keeps it, and the links read properly from then on.
- A name that went missing before any of this gets found again. Where it can be proved from what is already written down, it is worked out for free; where it cannot, the one that owns the name is simply asked, a few at a time, and never asked twice for the same silence.
