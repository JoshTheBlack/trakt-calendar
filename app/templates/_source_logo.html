{# How each service is SHOWN, in one place. Three surfaces need "which service is this, and what does it look like" — the outbound button on a card, the little logo beside a field two services disagreed about, and the same on the public share card. Written out at each of them, a third service would be three edits and one of them would be forgotten; here it is one. THE FILENAMES ARE NAMED HERE AND NOWHERE ELSE, and they are images rather than scripts or stylesheets, so they are not cache-busted assets — an image is fetched once and a changed one is a changed URL. `label` exists for the same reason as the picture: an alt attribute and a tooltip both have to say the service's name, and two spellings of it in two attributes is exactly the drift this file removes. TWO PICTURES PER SERVICE, AND WHICH ONE IS NOT A MATTER OF TASTE. A MARK is the service's symbol on its own disc or tile — it carries its own background, so it stays legible over poster art, over the near-black card and over anything else it is dropped on, and it reads at 10px where lettering would not. A WORDMARK is the service's NAME, and it is what a button gets: a button is a promise about where a click goes, and the name says that where a symbol asks to be recognised. So marks go on the flip control and the dual chips; wordmarks go on the outbound buttons and nowhere else. A MARK IS ALWAYS PART OF A CONTROL, and that is the rule that decides whether one is drawn at all. There used to be a third use — a badge naming the service that listed a card, drawn on every card from any service but the first. It looked exactly like the flip control it sat beside, so most posters carried something that asked to be clicked and did nothing when it was. The outbound button already names the service a card came from, so removing it cost no attribution. Anything new that wants to draw a mark has to be something a reader can act on. #} {% macro label(source) %}{{ 'Simkl' if source == 'simkl' else 'Trakt' }}{% endmacro %} {% macro mark(source, css='') %}{% endmacro %} {% macro wordmark(source) %}{% endmacro %} {# ONE OUTBOUND BUTTON, wearing the name of where it actually goes. Both card templates draw this and neither spells it: a card several services described gets one per service, and "what a link to a service looks like" then has one answer rather than one per page that happens to draw a card. The click is stopped from reaching the card underneath, which opens the details modal — following a link out and opening a panel about the same title are two different things to have asked for. #} {% macro link(source, url) %}{{ wordmark(source) }}{% endmacro %}
